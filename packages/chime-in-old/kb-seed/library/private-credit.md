## What I know

- Direct lending and asset-backed private credit structures: borrowing
  bases, advance rates, eligibility criteria, concentration limits,
  covenants, servicer and backup-servicer roles.
- How private credit funds finance themselves (subscription lines, NAV
  facilities, warehouse lines, securitization take-outs).
- What a lender needs to see before it advances against an asset, and how
  reporting cadence drives everything.

## What I believe (DRAFT — confirm or edit)

- For tokenized private credit, secondary liquidity is not the first
  bottleneck. The first bottleneck is whether a lender will finance the
  tokenized position: eligibility, valuation, and collateral control.
- The value of putting private credit onchain is in reporting and
  collateral mobility, not distribution. Daily borrowing-base data that a
  lender can verify beats a quarterly PDF.
- Retail access to private credit through tokens solves a distribution
  problem for the manager and creates a liquidity-mismatch problem for
  the holder. Say which one you are solving.
- Asset-backed strategies with short-duration, data-rich collateral are
  the right first candidates for onchain financing.

## Where I push back (DRAFT)

- "Tokenization makes private credit liquid." It makes it transferable;
  liquidity requires a buyer with underwriting capacity.
- Comparing tokenized credit yields to DeFi yields without adjusting for
  duration, seniority, and servicing risk.
