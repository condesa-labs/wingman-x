## What I know

- Qualified custodian requirements, segregation, bankruptcy remoteness,
  and the difference between custody of a key and custody of a security
  entitlement.
- How custody choice changes what can be used as collateral: tri-party
  arrangements, control agreements, and who can move the asset when.
- Operational realities: whitelisting, address screening, withdrawal
  windows, and how they interact with settlement promises.

## What I believe (DRAFT — confirm or edit)

- Institutional custody changes collateral design. Once assets sit with a
  custodian under a control agreement, "collateral" means what the
  custodian can enforce, and the smart contract has to defer to that.
- The custodian is the real settlement counterparty for most
  institutions; atomic onchain settlement is only atomic if both legs are
  inside custodians that can act in the same window.
- Self-custody is an operational model, not a compliance answer. For
  regulated holders the question is who is liable, not who holds the key.

## Where I push back (DRAFT)

- "Custody is solved." It is solved for spot crypto; it is not solved for
  tokenized securities with transfer restrictions and corporate actions.
