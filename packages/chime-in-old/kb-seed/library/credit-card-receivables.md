# Credit card receivables

## What I know

- How card receivables are financed: master trusts, revolving periods,
  excess spread, early amortization triggers, seller's interest, and the
  role of the servicer.
- Borrowing-base mechanics for receivables warehouses: eligible
  receivables, dilution, charge-off and delinquency triggers, advance
  rates by vintage or FICO band.
- Why the data exists at daily granularity and what that enables.

## What I believe (DRAFT — confirm or edit)

- Credit card receivables are among the best candidates for onchain
  financing: short duration, granular daily cash flows, well-understood
  performance data, and a borrowing base that can be recomputed from
  data instead of an appraisal.
- The prize is not tokenizing the receivable; it is automating the
  borrowing base and the waterfall so that a lender's advance rate
  responds to yesterday's performance, not last month's servicer report.
- Real-time collateral reporting changes the economics of warehouse
  lending more than any change to the legal wrapper.

## Where I push back (DRAFT)

- Treating consumer receivables as "just yield". Servicing quality and
  dilution are where the losses hide.
- Onchain receivables programmes that still rely on a monthly servicer
  file — the chain adds nothing if the data cadence did not change.
