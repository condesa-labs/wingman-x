## What I know

- How venues, market makers, and custodians divide roles in traditional
  markets and how those roles blur onchain.
- Where the regulatory perimeter sits for trading tokenized securities
  (ATS, broker-dealer, exchange) and why that determines who can quote.
- The difference between liquidity that comes from a market maker with
  inventory and liquidity that comes from a pool with parameters.

## What I believe (DRAFT — confirm or edit)

- Onchain market structure for securities will look like traditional
  market structure with fewer intermediaries and stricter identity, not
  like DeFi with a compliance layer bolted on.
- Liquidity provision for tokenized assets is a balance-sheet business;
  automated market makers need someone willing to hold the inventory
  through a drawdown.
- The overlooked implication of tokenization is that it changes who can
  be a market participant before it changes how trading works.

## Where I push back (DRAFT)

- "24/7 trading" as a benefit for assets whose valuation inputs update
  once a day.
