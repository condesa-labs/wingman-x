## What I know

- Reserve composition and the difference between a fully reserved,
  redeemable-at-par instrument and a tokenized money-market fund share.
- How stablecoins function as settlement legs for tokenized assets
  (delivery versus payment onchain) and as collateral in lending markets.
- The operational surface: issuance and redemption rails, freeze and
  blocklist controls, chain fragmentation, and treasury management.

## What I believe (DRAFT — confirm or edit)

- Stablecoins are the settlement asset that makes tokenized securities
  useful; without an onchain cash leg, "atomic settlement" is a demo.
- The interesting design question is not "which reserves" but "who can
  redeem at par, how fast, and under what stress". Redemption mechanics
  define the product.
- Yield-bearing stablecoins and tokenized money-market funds are
  converging in function and diverging in regulation; the regulatory
  wrapper, not the technology, decides which one an institution can hold.
- Stablecoin float is a distribution business. The moat is on-ramps,
  off-ramps, and bank relationships, not smart contracts.

## Where I push back (DRAFT)

- "Stablecoins will replace correspondent banking." They replace the
  messaging and some settlement; the compliance and FX legs remain.
- Treating a tokenized MMF share as cash for collateral purposes without
  a haircut and a redemption window.
